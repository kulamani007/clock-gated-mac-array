## Before you submit — things only you can supply

1. **Affiliation.** `main.tex` still contains a literal `[AFFILIATION TO BE SUPPLIED]`.
2. **Co-authors.** If a supervisor shaped this work, add them. A sole-authored brief from a personal email address attracts extra scrutiny.
3. **ORCID**, required by IEEE at submission.
4. **Make the repository public — at submission, not at acceptance.** The paper's acknowledgement claims the sources are released. Reviewers are exactly who needs that access, and TCAS-II review is single-blind so there is no anonymity reason to withhold it. One command:
   ```
   gh repo edit kulamani007/clock-gated-mac-array --visibility public --accept-visibility-change-consequences
   ```
   The repo is already relicensed to Apache-2.0 and cleaned of binaries in preparation. If you decide *not* to make it public, delete the reproducibility `\thanks` line from `main.tex` instead — do not leave a claim you have not honoured.

## Honest assessment of the odds

With the standard-cell results added I would now put this at roughly
**25–35 %**, up from the 15–25 % before the ASIC flow existed. Anticipate:

1. **"The baseline is the authors' own scheme."** Still the single biggest
   risk. Section III argues the exposure is general to any shared stage
   enable, and the negative control implements the *published* enable of [7]
   rather than a strawman — but expect it to be pressed.
2. **"Synthesis only."** No place and route and no static timing on the ASIC
   side, because no open STA tool was available. The paper says so plainly.
   This is the most tractable remaining gap.
3. **"Power confidence is Medium."** 12–13 % net annotation. Disclosed rather
   than hidden, but a referee may still consider it disqualifying in a power
   paper.
4. **"Scale is small."** Eight lanes, 16-bit.

### What would most raise the odds, in order

1. **Fix the SAIF annotation** so the DSP-packed variant gets a real power
   number instead of a lower bound. This is the weakest section of the paper.
2. **Add OpenSTA + OpenROAD place and route** on the sky130 netlists. The
   netlists already exist in `asic/netlist/`; this converts "synthesis only"
   into a full flow and would also give real ASIC timing and power.
3. **Scale the array** to 32 or 64 lanes.

## One thing I would not do

Do not oversell the headline. The honest framing is "these two techniques do
not compose, here is why and here is the fix", not "novel low-power
architecture". What makes the paper worth reading is the negative control, the
63 % result, and the two implementation cautions — not the gating scheme,
which is a competent engineering fix rather than a new architecture. Reviewers
respond well to papers that know the difference.
