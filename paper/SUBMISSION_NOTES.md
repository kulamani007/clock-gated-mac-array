## Before you submit — things only you can supply

1. **Affiliation.** `main.tex` has a literal `[AFFILIATION TO BE SUPPLIED]` placeholder in the `\thanks` block. Fill it in.
2. **Co-authors.** If a supervisor contributed to the direction of this work, add them. Sole-authored briefs from an unaffiliated address attract extra scrutiny.
3. **ORCID**, required at submission time by IEEE.
4. **Reproducibility link.** The paper claims sources are released. Put the repo somewhere public (GitHub) and cite the URL, or delete that `\thanks` line. Do not leave a claim you have not honoured.

## Submission mechanics

TCAS-II is submitted through ScholarOne Manuscripts, not by email. Select
"Brief" as the manuscript type. The 5-page limit is enforced on submission —
manuscripts over it are returned without review, so do not let the paper grow.

## Honest assessment of the odds

I would put this at roughly **15–25 % at TCAS-II**. It is a real, well-evidenced
result, but TCAS-II asks for work that "advances significantly the
state-of-the-art", and this is a composability caution plus two implementation
observations. Anticipate these referee objections:

1. **"The baseline is the authors' own scheme."** The hazard is exposed by
   completion-aware drain gating, which is not a published standard technique.
   Section III now argues the exposure is general to any shared stage enable,
   and the negative control reimplements the *published* enable of [7] rather
   than a strawman — but expect this to be pressed anyway. It is the single
   biggest risk to acceptance.
2. **"Scale is small."** Eight lanes, 16-bit, one FPGA family, no ASIC flow.
3. **"Power confidence is Medium."** Only 12–13 % of nets annotated. I flagged
   this in the paper rather than hiding it, which is the right call, but a
   referee may still consider it disqualifying for a power paper.
4. **"The async-reset/DSP result is a vendor guideline."** It is, and the paper
   says so and cites UG901. The claim made is only that it is *decisive* here.

### What would most raise the odds, in order

1. **Fix the SAIF annotation** so the DSP-packed variant gets a real power
   number instead of a lower bound. This removes objection 3 and converts the
   paper's weakest section into a strong one.
2. **Add an ASIC flow** (Yosys + OpenROAD on Sky130 is free) to show the result
   is not an artefact of DSP48 hard macros. This directly answers objection 2
   and is probably the highest-value addition after the SAIF fix.
3. **Scale the array** to 32 or 64 lanes.

### Fallback

If TCAS-II declines, **IEEE Embedded Systems Letters** is the natural next
target: 4 pages, no APC, and explicitly receptive to focused single-result
contributions of exactly this shape. The paper would need almost no
restructuring. I would not go to IEEE Access unless you are willing to pay the
APC.

## One thing I would not do

Do not oversell the headline. The honest framing is "these two techniques do
not compose, here is why and here is the fix", not "novel low-power
architecture". The negative control and the 63 % result are what make the paper
worth reading; the gating scheme itself is a competent engineering fix, not a
new architecture. Reviewers respond well to papers that know which is which.
